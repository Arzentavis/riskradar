# Assumptions log

Every assumption is set in one place, [`pipeline/config.py`](../pipeline/config.py), so the case can be re-run with different values. The business-case page on the dashboard lets the reader change the four business assumptions live.

| # | Assumption | Value | Why this value | Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Proactive call capacity per cycle | 20% of live book | Typical for a mid-size retail collections team | High. RiskRadar's lead grows as capacity shrinks (+86% at 5%, +10% at 50%) |
| A2 | Share of would-be defaults an early call prevents (cure rate) | 30% | Conservative end of the range reported for early-stage collections interventions | Scales loss prevented linearly; the *ranking* of strategies does not change |
| A3 | Loss given default (LGD) | 65% | Unsecured retail lending typically loses 60–75% of exposure after recoveries | Scales loss prevented linearly |
| A4 | Cost per proactive contact | ₹120 | Blended agent time, telephony and SMS | Low. Calls return about 80× their cost at the base case |
| A5 | Default definition | 90+ DPD within 12 months | Standard regulatory definition of a non-performing asset in India | Changing it would change the target |
| A6 | Exposure at default | Outstanding principal + current arrears | Simple and conservative; ignores interest accrual after the snapshot | Low |
| A7 | Accounts already 90+ DPD | Excluded, sent to recoveries | They have already defaulted; an early-warning call cannot prevent it | n/a |
| A8 | Protected attributes | Gender and age not used as inputs | Fair-treatment principle; used only to audit outcomes | n/a |

## About the data

The portfolio is **synthetic**: 60,000 borrowers generated by [`pipeline/generate_data.py`](../pipeline/generate_data.py). It was built to behave like Indian retail lending:

- Bureau scores on the 300–900 scale, with about 9% new-to-credit borrowers
- A 55% fixed-obligation-to-income (FOIR) underwriting cap, with about 4% policy exceptions up to 70%
- Gig workers and over-stretched borrowers are riskier, and the two effects interact
- About 7% of borrowers suffer a mid-history shock (job loss, medical, business slump)
- Raw feeds contain deliberate data-quality issues (missing incomes, negative tenure, inconsistent labels)
- Gender and age have **no direct effect** on default, so any gap the fairness audit finds comes from correlated features or business rules

Why synthetic: the public Home Credit dataset requires a Kaggle login, and real Indian lending data is confidential. The pipeline is written against generic tables, so swapping in a real feed means changing only the loader and the SQL column names.
